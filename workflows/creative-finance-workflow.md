# Creative Finance Workflow

## Goal

Teach and support the analysis of investor-style home acquisition methods while keeping strong guardrails around legality, ethics, title, insurance, lender rules, consumer protection, and professional review.

The agent must teach this path in Russian, but this workflow is written in English for internal use.

## Creative finance is advanced

The agent must present creative finance as an advanced path, not as a shortcut that eliminates risk.

Every scenario must be analyzed through:

- structure;
- source of funds;
- title status;
- existing debt;
- payment obligations;
- insurance;
- tax questions;
- default scenarios;
- exit strategy;
- professional review.

## Supported strategy categories

The agent may teach and analyze:

- seller financing;
- assumable loans when officially permitted;
- lease option;
- lease purchase;
- land contract or contract for deed;
- private money;
- hard money;
- DSCR-style investment financing;
- house hacking;
- renovation and refinance strategy;
- partnership structures;
- seller credits and rate buydown concepts;
- subject-to and wrap-style concepts only with strong risk framing and professional-review requirements.

## Required analysis template

For every creative finance opportunity, produce:

```markdown
## Creative finance review

### Strategy type

### What the user wants to achieve

### Known facts

### Assumptions

### Missing information

### Money flow

### Ownership and title questions

### Existing debt questions

### Insurance questions

### Tax/accounting questions

### Exit strategy

### Default scenario

### Professional reviews required

### Red flags

### Go / no-go status
```

## Phase 1: Education foundation

Teach in Russian:

- why sellers may accept non-standard terms;
- why investors use creative structures;
- difference between ownership, debt, control, possession, and option rights;
- why written agreements are not enough without title and professional review;
- how downside risk can exceed the initial cash invested.

Output:

- Russian glossary;
- risk checklist;
- comparison with classic purchase.

## Phase 2: Strategy selection

Classify the opportunity:

- owner financing;
- assumable financing;
- lease option;
- private money acquisition;
- renovation flip;
- house hack;
- other structure.

If the structure involves existing debt that remains connected to another party, require enhanced review and clear warnings.

## Phase 3: Seller and property intake

Collect:

- property address;
- seller motivation;
- asking price;
- requested down payment;
- proposed monthly payment;
- proposed term;
- balloon or payoff date;
- existing debt status if disclosed;
- taxes and insurance responsibility;
- repairs needed;
- occupancy status;
- title questions;
- reason seller is open to terms.

Output:

- seller conversation summary;
- property intake;
- missing documents list.

## Phase 4: Verification

Use browser and public records where available to check:

- ownership record;
- assessed value;
- tax history;
- recorded deed clues;
- recorded mortgage clues;
- public liens where available;
- permits and violations;
- flood or environmental clues;
- comparable sales;
- rent estimates when relevant.

Do not make final title conclusions. Title company or attorney review is required.

## Phase 5: Financial model

Build a simple model:

- purchase price;
- down payment;
- monthly payment;
- taxes;
- insurance;
- HOA;
- repairs;
- utilities where relevant;
- reserves;
- rent income if relevant;
- holding costs;
- exit costs;
- refinance or sale assumptions.

Output:

- monthly cash flow estimate;
- cash needed estimate;
- break-even point;
- stress test;
- exit strategy notes.

## Phase 6: Professional review package

Prepare questions for:

- real estate attorney;
- title company;
- lender or loan servicer when appropriate;
- insurance professional;
- CPA or tax professional;
- inspector or contractor.

Output:

- review package;
- document request list;
- go/no-go memo draft.

## Phase 7: Decision

Classify the deal:

- `Go`: no major unresolved red flags and professional review supports the path;
- `Conditional go`: possible only if listed issues are resolved;
- `No-go`: unacceptable risk, missing disclosures, bad economics, or professional review failure;
- `Learning only`: useful as an example, not actionable now.

## Red flags

Mark no-go or conditional no-go when:

- seller refuses title review;
- seller refuses attorney review;
- structure depends on secrecy;
- existing debt terms are unknown;
- payment servicing is unclear;
- insurance is unclear;
- user cannot survive a vacancy, repair, rate shock, or failed exit;
- purchase price does not make sense after repairs and exit costs;
- terms are not in writing;
- user is being rushed to pay non-refundable money.
